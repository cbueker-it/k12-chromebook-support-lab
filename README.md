**K-12 Chromebook Support Lab**

**Introduction**

Chromebooks are important in K-12 education environments because schools are increasingly becoming more tech-forward and dependent on endpoint devices for instruction, testing, communication, and day-to-day classroom work. Chromebooks can also provide schools with a practical and cost-conscious way to deploy and manage large numbers of student and staff devices.

Because of that, IT support staff need to understand how Chromebooks are provisioned, managed, supported, recovered, and eventually retired or reassigned.

This project documents my orientation to supporting Chromebooks in a K-12 IT environment. I used a free trial to access Google Admin Console, created an administrative account, explored the GUI, and familiarized myself with the areas of the interface most relevant to Chromebook and endpoint support.

I then mapped those concepts into a practical Chromebook device lifecycle. This process includes enrollment, assignment, policy application, deployment to the student or staff member, troubleshooting, recovery, reuse, and eventual retirement.

**Objectives**

- Understand the role of Google Admin Console in Chromebook management.
- Map the Chromebook lifecycle in a K-12 environment.
- Review device enrollment, assignment, centralized policy, applications, networking, and updates.
- Connect Chromebook troubleshooting and recovery to a practical endpoint-support workflow.
- Relate Chromebook administration to broader endpoint-management principles that also apply to Windows and other managed environments.

**Google Admin Console Orientation**

![Google Admin Console Orientation](images/01-google-admin-console-orientation.png)

This diagram reflects my orientation to the Google Admin Console and the areas of the dashboard that are most relevant to Chromebook support in a K-12 environment.

I focused on how device management, ChromeOS devices, managed browsers, applications, networks, and organizational structure fit together inside a centralized administrative platform.

The main takeaway is that Chromebook administration is built around centralized management rather than configuring every endpoint individually. This is a principle that carries across operating systems. In Windows environments, tools such as Microsoft Intune can also be used to centrally manage devices, policies, applications, security settings, and user access.

The administrative tools are different, but the larger endpoint-management principle is similar: organize the environment, apply consistent policy, validate the configuration, support the endpoint, and manage the device throughout its lifecycle.

**Chromebook Lifecycle Workflow**

![Chromebook Lifecycle Workflow](images/02-chromebook-lifecycle-workflow.png)

I created this workflow map to document my understanding of the lifecycle of a managed Chromebook in a K-12 school environment.

The process begins with identifying the need for a device. The Chromebook is then received, inspected, recorded in inventory, enrolled into the management environment, assigned to the appropriate organizational unit or user, configured through policy, and validated before deployment.

Once the endpoint is issued to a student, teacher, or staff member, it enters the support phase. This may include troubleshooting hardware, software, Wi-Fi, account access, applications, browser issues, or policy-related problems.

If needed, the device may be Powerwashed or recovered. At the end of its assignment, it can be prepared for reuse, reassigned, deprovisioned, or retired according to organizational policy.

Mapping this workflow helped me connect Google Admin concepts with practical, everyday IT support in a K-12 environment.

**Lifecycle**

Receive → Enroll → Assign → Apply → Validate → Deploy → Support → Recover → Reuse / Retire

**Lessons Learned**

- Chromebook administration, like Windows endpoint administration, is centered around centralized device and policy management.
- Organizational structure helps administrators apply appropriate settings, access, and restrictions to different users and devices.
- Chromebook support includes the entire endpoint lifecycle and is broader than break/fix troubleshooting alone.
- Powerwash and recovery are useful troubleshooting and recovery tools, but they fit within a larger managed-device process.
- K-12 IT support combines endpoint management, networking, user support, documentation, troubleshooting, and lifecycle planning.

**Summary**

This lab helped me connect Chromebook support with the broader endpoint lifecycle that I already use in my IT work:

**Configure → Validate → Deploy → Support → Recover → Document → Reuse or Retire**

The specific administrative tools may change between ChromeOS, Windows, Linux, or other environments, but the underlying IT support principles remain consistent.

The goal is to understand the endpoint, understand how it is centrally managed, support the user effectively, document the work, and maintain the device throughout its operational lifecycle.
